Documented analysis procedure

Preprocessing steps used before model fitting

Chapter 4 · analytic n = 468

The live checks above update from the configured dataset. The steps below summarize the preparation applied to the Chapter 4 analytic sample and the saved-model input pipeline.

!
How missingness was handled

Chapter 4 reports that the highest post-cleaning missingness was 3.20%, below the 5% per-feature limit. The missing values in mostUsedDrug and howManyTimesYouUsedLast30Days were structurally not applicable for respondents who had never used a substance. They were treated as not applicable, not filled in as evidence of use. For model predictors, the training pipeline records missingness flags for selected fields and imputes using saved training-set values: medians for flagged ordinal fields and modes for other ordinal fields.

  1. 01

    Check eligibility and internal consistency

    Responses were screened for valid values and logical agreement between lifetime use and recent use. One internally inconsistent record (never used, but reported recent use) was excluded when constructing the three-level outcome, leaving 468 records for Chapter 4 modelling.

  2. 02

    Standardise text and correct category errors

    Duplicate categories caused by typing variations were merged before encoding, including stronlyDisagree to stronglyDisagree, age band 30_36 to 30_35, and formallyEmployed to employed. The same corrections are applied by the prediction preprocessing code.

  3. 03

    Map ordered answers and preserve missingness

    Ordinal survey responses were mapped to ordered numeric codes. For designated predictor fields, a missingness indicator was created before imputation so the model can retain information that an answer was absent. “Prefer not to say” income is treated as missing by the training pipeline.

  4. 04

    Impute predictor values consistently

    Missing predictor values were filled from the training data rather than recalculated from an individual prediction. Selected fields use their saved median; other ordinal fields use their saved mode. The preprocessing artefacts store these values so training and live prediction use the same transformation.

  5. 05

    Encode categories and align model features

    Nominal text categories were one-hot encoded. Rare provinces and known duplicate labels were consolidated first. At prediction time, the generated columns are aligned to the saved training feature list; absent category columns are set to zero.

  6. 06

    Construct targets and prevent leakage

    The lifetime-use and recent-use targets are derived from their respective survey questions. Target fields, identifiers and post-outcome substance details are excluded from predictors so the model does not receive information that reveals the answer.

  7. 07

    Validate ranges and handle class imbalance during fitting

    Values were checked against valid ranges; no separate statistical outlier-removal step was applied. The Chapter 4 Random Forest used class_weight='balanced_subsample' for class imbalance. SMOTE was not used in the reported final modelling procedure.

Interpretation note: Chapter 4 identifies modal imputation and manual category correction as possible limitations. Imputation preserves records but can reduce variation, so results should be interpreted with that limitation in mind.

Documented in Chapter 4, Sections 4.2.2, 4.4, 4.8 and 4.9; predictor imputation and feature alignment follow the saved-model preprocessing pipeline.
